3. Seller pipeline

Two different offers, never mixed:
  • VALUE (seller side): a preliminary pricing opinion (comparative market analysis) based on recent comparable sales and Alexa's read of the property. It is not an appraisal; a lender, court or estate that needs a formal value requires a licensed appraiser. Optional add-on: an estimated seller net sheet (sale price minus estimated commission, transfer tax share, mortgage payoff and other liens, such as a VA partial claim). Estimates only.
  • PAYMENT (buyer side): an estimated monthly carrying cost for a specific home (principal and interest at an assumed rate, taxes, an insurance estimate, any HOA). It is not a loan approval, rate quote or Loan Estimate; only a lender provides those. Inactive while Friday's tour is on hold.

East Stroudsburg's Proposed 2027 Tax Rate: What Owners and Sellers Should Know Before Dec. 1

By Alexa Sanchez, Realtor® · Smart Way America Realty · East Stroudsburg, PA · Published November 9, 2026

Quick answer: East Stroudsburg Borough has proposed a 2027 borough tax rate of 10.2655 mills, up from 8.9654 mills in 2026. On a property assessed at $100,000, that's about $130 more a year for the borough's share of the bill. It isn't final: the public hearing and vote on Ordinance 1427 are set for Tuesday, December 1, 2026, at 7 p.m. For most owners it's a modest change. It's still worth understanding if you plan to sell or buy in the borough next year.

What the borough proposed

On October 26, 2026, Borough Council approved a proposed 2027 budget for advertising and posted a draft of Ordinance 1427, which would set the 2027 tax rate. Here's the proposed rate by fund, alongside the 2026 rates as published by the borough:

Fund2026 (mills, as published)2027 proposed (mills)
General Fund6.65447.9544
New Fire Apparatus0.05750.0575
Recreation0.44640.6064
Pension0.11070.1107
Debt Service1.69651.5365
Total levied8.9654*10.2655

*The borough's published 2026 fund rates add up to 8.9655 mills, while Ordinance 1419-25 and Monroe County's 2026 millage table list a levied total of 8.9654. The 0.0001-mill difference appears in the borough's own documents, most likely rounding. This article uses the levied total.

The General Fund, which pays for day-to-day borough operations, accounts for nearly all of the increase. Recreation rises, and debt service drops by the same amount.

What it means in dollars

A mill is $1 of tax for every $1,000 of assessed value. The proposed increase is about 1.3 mills, so:

  • $100,000 assessed value: about $130 more per year
  • $150,000 assessed value: about $195 more per year

Two cautions. First, use the assessed value printed on your tax bill, not an online estimate of your home's market value. In Monroe County those are different numbers, and mixing them up will overstate the change. Second, the borough is one piece of the bill. In 2026, East Stroudsburg's combined rate was 47.638614 mills: 33.01 for the school district, 8.9654 for the borough, 5.4773 for the county, and about 0.19 for the library. If only the borough rate changes, the combined rate rises by roughly 2.7%. The school district sets its rate separately.

Why the rate is going up

The proposed budget's millage table notes that it “assumes a 3% reduction in assessed values over 2026.” You can see that in the value of one mill: $432,288 in the proposed 2027 budget, compared with $445,658 in the final 2026 budget.

When the total assessed value in a town shrinks, each mill raises less money, so holding revenue steady takes a higher rate. That's arithmetic, not a verdict on the budget. It also means owners whose assessments haven't changed carry a slightly larger share. The posted budget has the fund-by-fund detail.

Water, sewer and trash

Water, sewer and trash rates are also part of the borough's 2027 budget process. Those charges come on separate bills and depend partly on usage. Check the borough's posted budget documents for the current proposed rates before you use any figure.

And then there's 2028

Monroe County's countywide reassessment is scheduled to take effect in January 2028. Reassessment resets assessed values across the county, and taxing bodies then set new rates. Nobody can tell you today what your 2028 bill will be. What's clear is that carrying costs for East Stroudsburg owners are moving in both 2027 and 2028.

What this means if you're selling in 2027

Here's my read. A change of this size won't decide whether your house sells or what it sells for. Condition, location, pricing against recent sales and the market at the time matter far more. But accuracy matters too:

  • Buyers ask what a house costs to own. Property taxes are part of the monthly housing cost a buyer budgets for and a lender reviews. A 2027 buyer will want 2027 numbers.
  • Give answers that hold up. Share your current-year taxes. After the Dec 1 vote, have the adopted 2027 borough rate ready too. Don't present a proposed rate as final, in either direction.
  • Keep two estimates separate. What you'd walk away with after the sale (your net proceeds) and what a buyer will pay each month to own the house (their carrying cost) are different calculations. Both are worth knowing; they shouldn't be blended.
  • Investors: update your assumptions. If a rental's numbers assumed flat municipal costs, re-run them once the 2027 rates and utility charges are adopted.

  • Attend Dec 1 or send comments. The public hearing is the formal moment for residents to weigh in.
  • Check relief programs. Eligible older adults, widows and widowers, and people with disabilities can apply for Pennsylvania's Property Tax/Rent Rebate. The deadline for 2025 taxes or rent is December 31, 2026. Veterans with a qualifying disability may want to read about Pennsylvania's disabled veteran property tax exemption.

Pennsylvania Disabled Veteran Property Tax Exemption: Who Qualifies Now and What HB 1257 Would Change

By Alexa Sanchez, Realtor® · Smart Way America Realty · East Stroudsburg, PA · Published November 11, 2026

Quick answer: Under current law, Pennsylvania fully exempts certain disabled veterans from real estate taxes on their principal dwelling. Eligibility requires a qualifying 100% service-connected rating from VA, wartime service, an honorable or under-honorable-conditions discharge, Pennsylvania residency, eligible ownership, and financial need. HB 1257 is a proposed constitutional amendment, not law. It would remove the wartime-service and financial-need requirements, among other changes. It passed the House in March 2026 and is pending in the Senate State Government Committee. If both chambers haven't approved the same text before the current General Assembly's term ends on November 30, 2026, the amendment process would have to start over in the 2027–28 session.

Part 1: Current law (what applies today)

Who qualifies today

The Pennsylvania Department of Military and Veterans Affairs (DMVA) lists these mandatory criteria for disabled veterans and their surviving spouses:

  • Residency: resident of the Commonwealth
  • Discharge: honorable or under-honorable-conditions discharge from the U.S. Armed Forces
  • Wartime service: service during a period of war as determined by VA (operation- or service-specific expeditionary medals can also establish wartime service)
  • Disability: a 100% permanent service-connected disability, total disability individual unemployability, or service-connected blindness, paraplegia, or loss of two or more limbs, as rated by VA
  • Principal dwelling: the property is occupied as the principal dwelling
  • Ownership: owned solely by the veteran, jointly with a spouse, or as an estate by the entirety
  • Financial need: determined by the State Veterans' Commission

How financial need works

On January 1, 2025, the State Veterans' Commission raised the presumptive need level to $114,637. Applicants at or below that level are presumed to have need. Applicants above it can still qualify by showing that allowable monthly expenses exceed monthly household income, with documentation. DMVA encourages veterans and surviving spouses previously denied for need to consider reapplying.

How to apply

DMVA directs applicants to the County Director of Veterans Affairs in the county where they live. In Monroe County, that's the county's veterans affairs office. Don't email completed applications or personal information to DMVA's program inbox; DMVA asks applicants not to. The current application form is dated October 2026; the form was revised, not the eligibility rules.

If you're buying and think you may qualify, raise it early with your agent and lender. An approved exemption changes your monthly housing cost.

Part 2: Proposed: HB 1257 (not law)

What HB 1257 would change

HB 1257 is a joint resolution proposing to amend Article VIII, Section 2(c) of the Pennsylvania Constitution. According to the House Appropriations Committee's fiscal note on the current version (Printer's No. 3060, March 24, 2026), the amendment would keep the full exemption for veterans with a 100% disability rating and:

  • Remove the requirement that the veteran served in a war or armed conflict
  • Remove the financial-need determination for veterans and their unmarried surviving spouses
  • Extend eligibility to the unmarried surviving spouse of a service member who died in the line of duty, was taken prisoner of war, or was declared missing in action
  • Allow a local government, by ordinance or resolution, to exempt veterans rated below 100% from any portion of their real property taxes (optional, not required)

The PN 3060 text also refers to veterans of a reserve component of the U.S. Armed Forces who are rated 100% permanent and total, or compensated at the 100% rate, by VA. Read the full bill text for the exact wording before relying on any summary, including this one.

  • March 23, 2026: amended on second consideration (Printer's No. 3060, the current text)
  • March 24, 2026: passed the House on final passage, 193–6
  • March 30, 2026: referred to the Senate State Government Committee. No Senate action since.

How a constitutional amendment passes in Pennsylvania (Article XI, Section 1)

  1. First passage: a majority of the members elected to each chamber (House and Senate) approve the amendment, with the votes recorded in each chamber's journal.
  2. First advertising: the Secretary of the Commonwealth publishes the proposed amendment in at least two newspapers in every county, three months before the next general election.
  3. Second passage: the General Assembly “next afterwards chosen,” meaning the next two-year General Assembly after that election, must approve the same amendment again by a majority of members elected to each chamber.
  4. Second advertising: the amendment is published again.
  5. Voters decide: it goes on a statewide ballot at least three months after second passage. It becomes part of the Constitution only if a majority of voters approve.

What November 30 actually means

Under Article II, Section 4, the General Assembly is a continuing body only for the two-year term its House members are elected to serve. The current term ends November 30, 2026, and legislation still pending then doesn't carry into the new term.

So far, HB 1257 has passed only the House. For first passage to be complete this term, the Senate must approve the same text. If the Senate changes it, the House must agree to those changes, all before November 30.

  • If that happens: the amendment would still need second passage in the 2027–28 General Assembly, advertising, and a statewide vote.
  • If it doesn't: a new resolution would have to be introduced, and the two-passage process would start over. First passage would come in 2027–28 at the earliest and second passage in 2029–30, before any ballot.

The fiscal note estimates the cost of the required newspaper advertising at $600,000 to $1 million from the General Fund. It doesn't estimate the effect on local tax revenue.

Either way, HB 1257 doesn't change today's rules. If you qualify under current law, apply under current law.

Before you choose a list price, check one more thing. Frequently asked questions

Do disabled veterans pay property taxes in Pennsylvania?
Some don't. Under current law, a veteran who meets every Department of Military and Veterans Affairs (DMVA) requirement can be fully exempt from real estate taxes on their principal dwelling. The requirements include a qualifying 100% service-connected rating, wartime service, an honorable or under-honorable-conditions discharge, eligible ownership, and financial need.

What is the income limit for Pennsylvania's disabled veteran tax exemption?
There isn't a hard cap. Since January 1, 2025, applicants with household income at or below $114,637 are presumed to have financial need. Applicants above that level can still qualify by documenting that allowable monthly expenses exceed monthly household income.

Is HB 1257 law?
No. HB 1257 is a proposed constitutional amendment. It passed the Pennsylvania House 193–6 on March 24, 2026, and was referred to the Senate State Government Committee on March 30, 2026. To reach the ballot, the same amendment must be approved by a majority of the members elected to each chamber in two consecutive General Assemblies, be advertised after each approval, and then be approved by voters. If both chambers have not approved the same text before the 2025–26 General Assembly's term ends on November 30, 2026, first passage is not complete, and the process would have to begin again with a new resolution in the 2027–28 session.

What would HB 1257 change?
According to the House Appropriations Committee fiscal note on Printer's No. 3060, it would keep the full exemption for veterans with a 100% rating while removing the wartime-service requirement and the financial-need determination. It would also extend eligibility to the unmarried surviving spouse of a service member who died in the line of duty, was taken prisoner of war, or was declared missing in action, and let local governments choose to exempt part of the taxes of veterans rated below 100%.

Does a VA partial claim have to be repaid when I sell my home?
Yes. VA states that a partial claim is repaid when the home is sold, refinanced, or the mortgage is paid off.
