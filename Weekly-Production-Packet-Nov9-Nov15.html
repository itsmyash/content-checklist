Don't quote a buyer last year's property taxes. Here's why that matters in East Stroudsburg right now. The borough just proposed its 2027 tax rate, and it's higher than this year's. That's not going to make or break your sale. But taxes are part of what a buyer budgets for every month, and the numbers are moving. The borough's budget assumes assessed values slip again, and the county reassessment lands in January 2028. So give buyers the number for the year they'll actually own it. East Stroudsburg's 2027 Property Tax Proposal: What Homeowners and Sellers Should Know

By Alexa Sanchez, REALTOR® · PA License No. RS326383 · Smart Way America Realty · 528 Seven Bridges Rd, Suite 108, East Stroudsburg, PA 18301 · (570) 688-8147 · Alexa@alexasanchez.net
Saturday, November 14, 2026

East Stroudsburg Borough has proposed raising its 2027 real estate tax rate from 8.9654 mills to 10.2655 mills. That works out to about $130 more a year for every $100,000 of assessed value, for the borough's share of the bill only. It isn't final. The public hearing and final vote are set for Tuesday, December 1, 2026, at 7 p.m.

What did East Stroudsburg propose for 2027?

Borough Council approved a proposed 2027 budget on October 26, 2026. Draft Ordinance 1427 would set the borough's real estate tax rate for 2027, and the hearing and final vote are scheduled for December 1 at 7 p.m. The notice is posted on the borough's public hearings page.

A note on the numbers: the borough's proposed 2027 fund rates add up to 10.2655 mills, the figure used here. Some summaries round it to 10.2654. The 2026 rate is 8.9654 mills, the rate set by ordinance and shown in Monroe County's 2026 millage table. Check the final ordinance after December 1.

The same budget also proposes changes to water, sewer and trash rates. Those charges depend on your account and usage, so check the borough's budget documents for the details.

How much would my East Stroudsburg tax bill go up?

One mill is $1 of tax for every $1,000 of assessed value. Here's the borough's share at three assessed values:

Assessed value2026 borough tax (8.9654 mills)2027 proposed (10.2655 mills)Difference
$100,000$896.54$1,026.55+$130.01
$150,000$1,344.81$1,539.83+$195.02
$200,000$1,793.08$2,053.10+$260.02

That's about a 14.5% increase in the borough's own rate. But the borough rate is only part of your total bill. In 2026, East Stroudsburg's combined rate (school, county and borough) was 47.638614 mills, according to Monroe County's 2026 millage table. The borough's share was about 19% of that.

If the school and county rates stayed the same, the proposed borough increase would raise the combined rate by about 2.7%. The school district and the county set their own rates on their own schedules, so your full 2027 bill depends on their decisions too.

Why is the borough raising its rate?

The borough's budget materials point to reassessment appeals reducing the borough's total assessed value, and the proposed budget assumes assessed values decline again in 2027. When the tax base shrinks and costs don't, the rate has to rise to collect the same amount. That shifts more of the total onto properties whose assessments didn't go down.

That's arithmetic, not a judgment about anyone's appeal. Every owner is entitled to a fair assessment.

Is assessed value the same as what my house is worth?

No. Assessed value is the county's taxable value, set during Monroe County's last countywide reassessment, which was implemented in 2020. It isn't a market value or an appraisal. A $200,000 assessment doesn't mean a home would sell for $200,000.

I'm selling in 2027. What should I tell buyers about taxes?

  • Give them the current bill, and say plainly that the 2027 borough rate is proposed until December 1, and final after that.
  • Don't quote a proposed rate as final. Update your numbers once Council votes.
  • Keep it in proportion. A change of about $130 a year per $100,000 of assessed value is unlikely to decide a sale on its own. Buyers and lenders look at the whole monthly payment, including taxes, so accurate numbers prevent surprises late in the deal.
  • Mention the reassessment. New county values take effect in January 2028, so a buyer's future bill may be based on a different assessed value than yours.

What does the 2028 reassessment mean for East Stroudsburg taxes?

Monroe County's countywide reassessment takes effect in January 2028. According to Tyler Technologies, the county's contractor, informal review meetings will run from January to May 2027 by appointment, with scheduling details included in each owner's tentative value letter.

Pennsylvania law limits what taxing bodies can collect in the first year of a reassessment. Under 53 Pa.C.S. §8823, as amended by Act 156 of 2016, each taxing district must first set a rate that brings in the same total revenue as the year before. It can then raise total revenue by up to 10% only by a separate vote. Individual bills can still go up or down depending on how each property's new value compares with others.

How can East Stroudsburg residents weigh in?

Attend the public hearing on Tuesday, December 1, at 7 p.m. That's where residents can comment before Council takes its final vote. The proposed ordinance and the hearing notice are on the borough's website. The borough office is at 24 Analomink Street, East Stroudsburg.

Pennsylvania's Disabled Veteran Property Tax Exemption: Who Qualifies Today and What HB 1257 Would Change

By Alexa Sanchez, REALTOR® · PA License No. RS326383 · Smart Way America Realty · 528 Seven Bridges Rd, Suite 108, East Stroudsburg, PA 18301 · (570) 688-8147 · Alexa@alexasanchez.net
Saturday, November 14, 2026

Pennsylvania already exempts certain 100% disabled veterans from real estate taxes on the home they live in. To qualify today, a veteran needs a qualifying VA disability rating, wartime service, an honorable discharge, Pennsylvania residency, eligible ownership of their principal dwelling and financial need. HB 1257 would loosen those rules, but it's a proposal, not law.

Part 1: Current law (what applies today)

Who qualifies for Pennsylvania's disabled veteran real estate tax exemption?

The Pennsylvania Department of Military and Veterans Affairs (DMVA) lists these mandatory criteria for disabled veterans and their surviving spouses:

  • Residency: a resident of Pennsylvania
  • Discharge: honorable or under-honorable-conditions discharge from the U.S. Armed Forces
  • Wartime service: service during a period of war as determined by VA (operation- or service-specific expeditionary medals can also establish wartime service)
  • Disability: a 100% permanent service-connected disability, total disability individual unemployability, or service-connected blindness, paraplegia, or loss of two or more limbs, as rated by VA
  • Principal dwelling: the veteran lives in the property as their principal home
  • Ownership: owned solely by the veteran, jointly with a spouse, or as an estate by the entirety
  • Financial need: determined by the State Veterans' Commission

How does the financial need test work?

On January 1, 2025, the State Veterans' Commission raised the presumptive need level to $114,637. Applicants at or below that level are presumed to have need. Applicants above it can still qualify by documenting that allowable monthly expenses exceed monthly household income. DMVA encourages veterans and surviving spouses previously denied for need to consider reapplying.

How do I apply in Monroe County?

DMVA directs applicants to the County Director of Veterans Affairs in the county where they live. In Monroe County, that's the county's veterans affairs office. The current application form is dated October 2026. The form was revised; the eligibility rules weren't.

If you're buying a home and think you may qualify, raise it early with your agent and lender. An approved exemption changes your monthly housing cost.

Part 2: HB 1257 (proposed, not law)

What would HB 1257 change?

HB 1257 is a joint resolution proposing to amend Article VIII, Section 2(c) of the Pennsylvania Constitution. According to the House Appropriations Committee's fiscal note on the current version (Printer's No. 3060), it would keep the full exemption for veterans with a 100% rating and:

  • Remove the requirement that the veteran served during a war or armed conflict
  • Remove the financial-need determination for veterans and their unmarried surviving spouses
  • Extend eligibility to the unmarried surviving spouse of a service member who died in the line of duty, was a prisoner of war, or was declared missing in action
  • Let local governments choose, by ordinance or resolution, to exempt veterans rated below 100% from part of their real property taxes

How does a constitutional amendment become law in Pennsylvania?

Under Article XI of the Pennsylvania Constitution, an amendment takes five steps:

  1. First passage: both chambers approve the same text.
  2. First advertising: the amendment is published.
  3. Second passage: the next General Assembly approves the same text again.
  4. Second advertising: it's published again.
  5. Voters decide: it goes on a statewide ballot at least three months after second passage and becomes part of the Constitution only if a majority approves.

What does November 30 mean for HB 1257?

The current General Assembly's term ends November 30, 2026, and legislation still pending then doesn't carry over. So far, HB 1257 has passed only the House. For first passage to be complete this term, the Senate must approve the same text before November 30. If the Senate changes it, the House has to agree to those changes by then too.

  • If that happens, the amendment would still need second passage in the 2027–28 General Assembly, advertising, and a statewide vote.
  • If it doesn't, a new resolution would have to be introduced and the process would start over: first passage in 2027–28 at the earliest, second passage in 2029–30, then a ballot.

Either way, HB 1257 doesn't change today's rules. If you qualify under current law, apply under current law.
