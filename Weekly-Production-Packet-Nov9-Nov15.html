East Stroudsburg's Proposed 2027 Tax Rate: What Owners and Sellers Should Know Before Dec. 1

By Alexa Sanchez, Realtor® · Smart Way America Realty · East Stroudsburg, PA · Published November 9, 2026

Quick answer: East Stroudsburg Borough has proposed a 2027 real estate tax rate of 10.2655 mills, up from 8.9654 mills in 2026. On a property assessed at $100,000, that's about $130 more per year for the borough's portion of the bill. It is not final: the public hearing and vote on Ordinance 1427 are set for Tuesday, December 1, 2026, at 7 p.m.

What the borough proposed

Borough Council approved a proposed 2027 budget at a special meeting on October 26, 2026, and posted a draft of Ordinance 1427, which sets the 2027 tax rate. Here's how the proposed rate breaks down by fund, compared with 2026:

Fund2026 (mills)2027 proposed (mills)
General Fund6.65447.9544
New Fire Apparatus0.05750.0575
Recreation0.44640.6064
Pension0.11070.1107
Debt Service1.69651.5365
Total8.965410.2655

Most of the increase lands in the General Fund, which pays for day-to-day borough operations. Debt service actually goes down slightly.

What it means in dollars

A mill is $1 of tax for every $1,000 of assessed value. The proposed increase is about 1.3 mills, so:

  • $100,000 assessed value: about $130 more per year
  • $150,000 assessed value: about $195 more per year

Two cautions. First, use the assessed value on your tax bill, not your home's market value. In Monroe County, those are very different numbers, and plugging in your Zillow estimate will overstate the change. Second, the borough is only one piece of your bill. In 2026, East Stroudsburg's combined rate was 47.638614 mills: 33.01 for the school district, 8.9654 for the borough, 5.4773 for the county and about 0.19 for the library. If only the borough rate changes, the combined rate rises about 2.7%, not the 14.5% you'd get by looking at the borough line alone. The school district sets its own rate on a separate schedule.

Why the rate is going up: the base is shrinking

The proposed budget's millage table says it “assumes a 3% reduction in assessed values over 2026.” You can see it in the value of one mill: the 2027 budget puts it at $432,288, compared with $445,658 in the borough's 2026 budget materials.

When the total assessed value in a town drops and spending doesn't, the rate has to rise to raise the same money. That's arithmetic, not a judgment about the budget. It also means the burden shifts. If your assessment stayed the same while others went down, a larger share of the borough's budget falls on you.

Water, sewer and trash are part of the same budget

The 2027 budget process also covers the borough's water, sewer and trash rates. Those charges show up on separate bills and depend partly on usage, so they're harder to sum up in one number. Check the borough's posted budget documents for the current proposed rates before you put a figure in a buyer's cost estimate or your own budget.

And then there's 2028

Monroe County's countywide reassessment is scheduled to take effect in January 2028. Reassessment resets assessed values across the county, and municipalities and school districts then adjust their rates. Nobody can tell you today what your 2028 bill will be, and anyone who claims to know is guessing. What we do know: 2027 and 2028 are both years where carrying costs for East Stroudsburg owners are in motion.

What this means if you're selling in 2027

Here's what I'm seeing, and why I'd raise this with any East Stroudsburg seller:

  • Buyers qualify on the full payment. A lender looks at principal, interest, taxes and insurance together. If the 2027 rate passes, a buyer's tax line goes up, and that buyer's price ceiling comes down a little with it.
  • Price for the buyer who actually exists. Your 2026 bill is history. A 2027 buyer will be budgeting for 2027 costs, and a well-prepared buyer's agent will look them up.
  • Disclose what's known, plainly. Share your current-year taxes, and once the borough votes on Dec 1, have the adopted 2027 rate ready. Don't present a proposed rate as final, in either direction.
  • Investors: re-run the numbers. If you own a rental in the borough and your math assumed flat costs, update it with the 2027 rate and the utility changes before you decide whether to hold or sell.

What this means if you're staying

  • Read the proposal. The proposed budget and draft Ordinance 1427 are posted on the borough's 2027 budget page.
  • Go on Dec 1, or send comments. The public hearing is the formal moment for residents to weigh in before the vote.
  • Know your relief options. If you're an older adult, a widow or widower, or a person with a disability with limited income, check whether you qualify for Pennsylvania's Property Tax/Rent Rebate. Applications for 2025 taxes or rent are due December 31, 2026 (PA Department of Revenue).

Frequently asked questions

Is East Stroudsburg raising property taxes in 2027?
The borough has proposed it. The proposed 2027 rate is 10.2655 mills, up from 8.9654. It becomes final only if Council adopts Ordinance 1427, with the hearing and vote scheduled for December 1, 2026.

How much will my tax bill go up?
For the borough's portion only, about $130 a year per $100,000 of assessed value. Your school and county taxes are set separately.

Does this include school taxes?
No. The East Stroudsburg Area School District sets its own millage, which is the largest part of the bill (33.01 mills on the 2026 county table).

Why would the rate go up if the borough isn't spending wildly more?
Partly because the tax base is shrinking. The proposed budget assumes assessed values fall 3% from 2026, so each mill raises less money.

January 2028, according to the county's reassessment project page.

Pennsylvania Disabled Veteran Property Tax Exemption: Who Qualifies Now and What HB 1257 Would Change

By Alexa Sanchez, Realtor® · Smart Way America Realty · East Stroudsburg, PA · Published November 11, 2026

Quick answer: Pennsylvania already offers a full real estate tax exemption on the primary home of certain disabled veterans. To qualify today, a veteran needs a qualifying 100% service-connected disability rating, wartime service, and a finding of financial need by the State Veterans' Commission. HB 1257, a proposed constitutional amendment, would broaden eligibility. It passed the state House in March 2026 but has not passed the Senate, and it expires when the legislative session ends November 30, 2026, unless the Senate acts.

Who qualifies today

According to the Pennsylvania Department of Military and Veterans Affairs (DMVA), mandatory criteria for the Disabled Veterans' Real Estate Tax Exemption include:

  • Service during a period of war, as determined by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (operation- or service-specific expeditionary medals can also establish wartime service)
  • A 100% permanent service-connected disability, total disability individual unemployability, or service-connected blindness, paraplegia, or loss of two or more limbs, as rated by VA
  • The property is occupied as the principal dwelling
  • Pennsylvania residency
  • Financial need, as determined by the State Veterans' Commission

On financial need: on January 1, 2025, the State Veterans' Commission raised the presumptive need level to $114,637. DMVA encourages veterans and surviving spouses who were previously denied for need to consider reapplying. Surviving spouses of eligible veterans may also qualify; check the DMVA criteria for the details that apply to them.

DMVA's application form was revised in October 2026. The form changed; the program's eligibility rules did not.

How to apply

Start with the DMVA's Real Estate Tax Exemption page and the current application form. Your county's veterans affairs office can help you gather documentation, including your VA rating decision and proof of wartime service. If you're buying a home and think you may qualify, raise it early with your agent and your lender, because an approved exemption changes your monthly payment.

What HB 1257 would change

HB 1257 is a joint resolution proposing an amendment to the Pennsylvania Constitution's provisions on tax exemptions. Bill summaries describe it as broadening who qualifies, centered on veterans rated 100% permanently and totally disabled, and adding a local option for partial exemptions at lower disability ratings. Read the bill text on palegis.us for the exact language before relying on any summary, including this one.

Its path so far:

  • Introduced April 17, 2025
  • Passed the House on March 24, 2026
  • Referred to the Senate State Government Committee on March 30, 2026, where it remains as of November 8, 2026

Why November 30 matters

Pennsylvania's 2025–26 legislative session ends November 30, 2026. Bills that haven't passed by then expire and would have to be reintroduced in the new session.

Even a Senate vote this month wouldn't make HB 1257 law. A constitutional amendment must pass both chambers in identical form in two consecutive sessions, then win approval from voters in a statewide election. So the realistic timeline, even on the fastest path, runs through the 2027–28 session before it could reach the ballot.

Translation: if you qualify under today's rules, apply under today's rules. Don't wait for HB 1257.

Three things that get confused

  • “HB 1257 passed.” It passed one chamber. It is not law.
  • VA's homelessness grants. VA's September 2026 grants to Pennsylvania organizations fund homelessness services. They are not homebuyer or closing-cost money.
  • Veterans' Trust Fund grants. DMVA's Veterans' Trust Fund grant round (applications Nov. 2–Dec. 18, 2026) is for organizations and county programs, not individual buyers.

If you're a veteran thinking about selling

One more thing to check before you set a price: if your VA servicer helped you through a hardship with a VA partial claim, that balance is repaid when the home is sold, refinanced or paid off. It belongs in your net-proceeds estimate from the start. VA has required servicers to fully implement the partial claim program by November 28, 2026. Ask your servicer for your current balance.

If you're selling to buy again with a VA loan, VA's funding fee chart is unchanged this fall (still labeled effective April 7, 2023). VA also launched a campaign with the National Association of REALTORS® on November 7 encouraging agents to ask every client, “Are you a Veteran?” It doesn't change any rules, but it's a good reminder to say so early.

Frequently asked questions

Do Pennsylvania disabled veterans pay property taxes?
Some don't. Veterans who meet DMVA's criteria, including a qualifying 100% service-connected rating, wartime service and financial need, can be fully exempt from real estate taxes on their principal residence.

What is the income limit for Pennsylvania's disabled veteran tax exemption?
Since January 1, 2025, the presumptive need level is $114,637. Applicants above that level can still try to show need, but it's no longer presumed.

Is HB 1257 law?
No. It passed the House in March 2026 and has been in Senate committee since March 30. It expires November 30, 2026 unless the Senate acts. Even if it passes, it needs a second passage in the next session and a statewide vote.

Does a VA partial claim have to be paid back when I sell?
Yes. The partial claim is repaid when the home is sold, refinanced or paid off.
