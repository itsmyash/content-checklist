East Stroudsburg's 2027 Tax Proposal: The 14.5% Headline vs. Your Actual Bill
The Pocono Ledger · Week of November 8, 2026 · Alexa Sanchez, REALTOR®

The short version. East Stroudsburg Borough has proposed raising its 2027 real estate tax rate from 8.9654 mills to 10.2655 mills. Council holds a public hearing on Ordinance 1427-26 on Tuesday, Dec. 1, at 7 p.m., and can adopt it that night. It isn't final yet.

The headline number. The borough's own levy would rise about 14.5%, and it would be the third increase in a row. The rate was 7.9654 mills in 2025. But the borough is not most of your bill. Under the county's 2026 millage table, a property in the borough pays 47.638614 mills in total, and 33.01 of those go to the East Stroudsburg Area School District. The borough's share is under a fifth.

What it costs. The extra 1.3 mills is $130 a year for every $100,000 of assessed value. On a $100,000 assessment, the 2026 county, library, borough and school rates add up to about $4,764 before any homestead exclusion, so the proposal adds roughly 2.7%. One warning: your assessed value is printed on your tax bill, and it is not what your house would sell for. Don't run this math on a sale price.

What's not on the tax notice. Water, sewer and trash. The borough's October budget packet proposes water up 15.75%, sewer up 6% and trash up 9.5%. Its own example shows tax plus utilities rising from $3,759 to $4,239 a year. Your actual usage changes that figure.

Why this is happening. The packet says assessment appeals cut the borough's taxable value, and property-tax revenue fell 10.8% last year. That's how millage works: when some owners win lower assessments, the base shrinks, and the rate rises for everyone who didn't appeal. Regional police costs are part of the pressure, too.

What I'm watching. Stroud Township is reworking how it splits regional police costs, and the borough expects Stroudsburg to raise its rates as well. Monroe County's countywide reassessment is scheduled to take effect in January 2028. For Stroud-area owners, that's more than a year of moving numbers.

If you're buying in the borough. Ask for the current tax bill, and budget for the 2027 rate if it passes. Ask about water and sewer charges too, not just taxes.

If you're selling in 2027. Buyers shop by monthly payment, and taxes and utilities sit inside that payment. Pull your current bills now, and know the Dec. 1 outcome before you set a price. A cost a buyer hears from you up front is a fact; one they discover mid-negotiation becomes a discount request.
