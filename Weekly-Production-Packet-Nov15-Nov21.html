83 Monroe County Listings Expired in October. Here's What They Had in Common

By Alexa Sanchez, REALTOR® · PA License No. RS326383 · Smart Way America Realty · 528 Seven Bridges Rd, Suite 108, East Stroudsburg, PA 18301 · (570) 688-8147 · Alexa@alexasanchez.net The Pocono Ledger · Monday, November 16, 2026

The number: 83 Monroe County homes reached the end of their listing agreements in October without selling. In the same month, 206 homes closed. That's about four expired listings for every ten sales.

What happened

I pulled every Monroe County residential listing that expired in October from the Pocono MLS and compared them with the 206 that sold. Three patterns stood out.

They had already cut their price. 53 of the 83 (64%) had reduced their price at least once before the listing ran out. Among homes that sold, 76 of 206 (37%) had a cut. So the price cut wasn't the difference. Most of the expired homes cut and still didn't sell.

They sat for a long time. The median expired listing had been on the market 173 days, nearly six months. The median home that sold had been on the market 36 days.

They were spread across every price range. 12 were under $250,000, 41 between $250,000 and $399,999, 13 between $400,000 and $499,999, and 17 at $500,000 or more. The middle of the market, where most Monroe homes trade, had the most.

What it means

Buyers were there. 206 homes sold in October at a median of $350,000, according to the Pocono Mountains Association of REALTORS®. The expired listings weren't in a market with no buyers. They were in a market where buyers had other choices and picked them.

The timeline tells most of the story. A home that's priced where buyers expect usually gets its best attention in the first few weeks. When it doesn't, the seller cuts, and buyers who have watched it for months read the cut as a reason to wait for another. By month six, the listing is stale even at a fair number.

Who it affects

  • Homeowners planning to list this winter or spring. You'll be compared with inventory that's 4.6% higher than last year.
  • Owners whose listing just expired. Relisting the same house at the same price, with the same photos, usually repeats the result.

Behind on a VA Loan? Ask Your Servicer About the Partial Claim Before November 28

By Alexa Sanchez, REALTOR® · PA License No. RS326383 · Smart Way America Realty · 528 Seven Bridges Rd, Suite 108, East Stroudsburg, PA 18301 · (570) 688-8147 · Alexa@alexasanchez.net The Pocono Ledger · Tuesday, November 17, 2026

The date: November 28, 2026. That's the deadline for mortgage servicers to have the VA's new Partial Claim Program fully built into their systems.

What happened

The VA Home Loan Program Reform Act became law on July 30, 2025. It authorized a VA Partial Claim Program, which the VA launched on June 15, 2026. Servicers could start using it right away but were given until November 28 to finish implementing it, according to the U.S. Government Accountability Office's November 2026 report on the program.

How it works

Based on the VA's description at launch:

  1. Your mortgage servicer, the company you send your payment to, reviews veterans who are behind to see if they may qualify.
  2. A qualifying veteran goes on a three-month trial payment plan.
  3. If you complete the trial, the servicer pays the overdue amount to bring your loan current, and the VA pays the servicer back.
  4. That amount becomes a separate lien on your home. It's repaid when the loan is paid off, refinanced, or the home is sold.

The partial claim is one of several options. The VA also lists repayment plans and loan modifications, including 30- and 40-year modifications. Your servicer decides which ones fit your situation.

What it means

Before this program existed, a veteran who was seriously behind had fewer ways to catch up without changing the loan's terms. For some, selling seemed like the only way out. The National Association of REALTORS® noted the program is expected to reduce forced sales.

Until November 28, access depends on the servicer. Some have been processing partial claims since summer. Others may still be finishing the setup.

Who it affects

  • Veterans in Monroe County with a VA-guaranteed loan who have missed payments.
  • Family members who help a veteran parent or relative manage their finances.
  • Veterans who already got a partial claim and are now thinking about selling. The lien gets paid off from your sale proceeds at closing, so it belongs in your seller net sheet from day one.

What to do

  1. Call your servicer. Ask to be reviewed for VA home retention options, including the partial claim. Write down who you spoke with and when.
  2. Ask whether they're processing partial claims yet. If not, ask what they can offer in the meantime and when it will be available.
  3. Don't list in a panic first. Selling may still be the right choice for some people, but it should be a decision, not a reaction. If you do decide to sell, a Realtor and a settlement company should know about any partial-claim lien from the start.
  4. You can also contact the VA directly. VA loan technicians help veterans who are behind; the contact information is on VA.gov under home loans.

Misconception to correct

"If I'm behind on my VA loan, I have to sell." Not necessarily. Talk to your servicer about every option first.

The Median Rose 9.4%. Your House Probably Didn't.

By Alexa Sanchez, REALTOR® · PA License No. RS326383 · Smart Way America Realty · 528 Seven Bridges Rd, Suite 108, East Stroudsburg, PA 18301 · (570) 688-8147 · Alexa@alexasanchez.net The Pocono Ledger · Wednesday, November 18, 2026

The number: Monroe County's median sale price in October was $350,000, up 9.4% from $320,000 a year earlier, according to the Pocono Mountains Association of REALTORS®.

It's a real number. It just doesn't mean what most people will assume it means.

What happened

In the same month, Monroe County closings fell 18.8%, from 250 to 203. When fewer homes sell, the median depends heavily on which ones did.

The Pocono MLS-wide data (all counties the MLS covers, not only Monroe) shows where the drop came from:

  • Sales under $250,000: 87 in October, down from 136 a year earlier (−36%)
  • Sales of $400,000 to $499,999: 58, up from 50
  • Sales of $500,000 and up: 58, down slightly from 61

The cheapest part of the market shrank the most. Higher-priced sales held steady or grew. When the bottom of the list gets shorter, the middle of the list moves up, even if no individual house gained a dollar.

What it means

Some of October's 9.4% is real appreciation. Some of it is a different mix of homes selling. The data can't tell us exactly how much is which, but a few comparisons suggest the month overstates the trend:

  • The year-to-date median is $339,000, up 7.6%, a broader read across 1,910 sales.
  • Monthly medians have bounced this year, from $370,000 in April to $336,125 in September.
  • The affordability index fell from 121 to 101, meaning buyers have less room to pay more.

So if your home was worth about $320,000 last October, it's probably worth more today. But "add 9.4%" isn't how to find out.

Who it affects

  • Sellers setting a list price. Pricing off a headline percentage is the fastest way to end up in next month's expired-listing count.
  • Owners checking online estimates. Automated values react to recent sales. With fewer sales and a shifted mix, they can swing more than usual.
  • Buyers. A higher median doesn't mean every home is 9% pricier. Compare price per square foot and condition within your range.

What to do

  1. Use year-to-date numbers, not one month.
  2. Price from homes like yours, in your town and price range, sold in the last few months.
  3. Check what you'd compete against today. In October there were 923 homes for sale in Monroe County, 4.6% more than a year ago.

Misconception to correct

"Prices went up 9.4%, so my house is worth 9.4% more." The median measures the middle sale in a month. It doesn't measure your house.

Is It a Good Time to Sell a House in the Poconos? (Monroe County, November 2026)

By Alexa Sanchez, REALTOR® · PA License No. RS326383 · Smart Way America Realty · 528 Seven Bridges Rd, Suite 108, East Stroudsburg, PA 18301 · (570) 688-8147 · Alexa@alexasanchez.net Tuesday, November 17, 2026

Short answer: It can be, if your price fits today's competition. Monroe County home prices are still higher than a year ago (the October median was $350,000), but fewer homes are selling, more are listed, and homes are taking longer to sell. Well-priced homes still sell fast. Overpriced ones sit and expire.

What does the Monroe County housing market look like right now?

The Pocono Mountains Association of REALTORS® October 2026 report for Monroe County shows a market with more choice for buyers than a year ago:

October 2026 Value vs. October 2025
Median sale price $350,000 +9.4%
Homes sold 203 −18.8%
New listings 346 +6.8%
Homes for sale 923 +4.6%
Months of supply 4.7 up from 4.4

Share of original list price received 95.2% up from 94.6%

So prices held up, but activity slowed. Buyers have more homes to compare, and fewer of them signed contracts this fall: pending sales were below last year in July, August, September and October.

Are home prices still going up in the Poconos?

Yes, but less than October's 9.4% headline suggests. The year-to-date median through October was $339,000, up 7.6% from $315,000. A single month can swing. In 2026 the monthly median ranged from $300,000 in February to $370,000 in April.

October's jump also reflects which homes sold. Across the Pocono MLS, sales under $250,000 fell sharply from a year earlier while higher-priced sales held steady, and that pushes the median up on its own.

The practical point for sellers: your home's value comes from homes like yours, near you, that sold recently, not from a countywide percentage.

How long does it take to sell a house in Monroe County?

Average days on market in October was 58, up from 49 a year earlier. The average hides a split, though. Looking at Pocono MLS records for October closings in Monroe County:

  • 93 of 203 homes sold within 30 days on market.
  • 38 took more than 90 days.
  • Homes that sold above list price had been on the market a median of 11 days. Homes that sold below list had been on about 50.

Meanwhile, 83 Monroe County listings expired in October without selling, and their median time on the market was 173 days.

In other words, homes tend to sell quickly or not at all. The first few weeks matter most.

Does my price range matter?

A lot. Comparing active listings in mid-November with October sales:

  • Under $250,000: about 3.4 months of supply; homes that sold had a median of 26 days on market.
  • $250,000–$399,999: about 4.4 months; the most competition, with 392 homes for sale.
  • $400,000–$499,999: about 3.7 months.
  • $500,000 and up: about 5.9 months, the slowest range, and the one where new listings are growing fastest across the Pocono MLS.

Buying an Older Home in the Poconos: What to Check in a 1970s or '80s House

By Alexa Sanchez, REALTOR® · PA License No. RS326383 · Smart Way America Realty · 528 Seven Bridges Rd, Suite 108, East Stroudsburg, PA 18301 · (570) 688-8147 · Alexa@alexasanchez.net Thursday, November 19, 2026

Short answer: Most homes for sale in the Poconos are older. Half of the homes that sold in Monroe County in October 2026 were built before 1990. When you buy one, plan inspections beyond the standard home inspection: radon, well water, septic, heating fuel and tanks, electrical, plumbing and roof age. Then read the seller's disclosure carefully.

How old are the homes selling in Monroe County?

Older than many buyers expect. In Pocono MLS records for October 2026 Monroe County closings:

  • The median year built was 1989.
  • 106 of 206 homes sold were built before 1990.
  • Only 10 were built in 2010 or later.

Much of the Poconos grew as a second-home and vacation-community market in the 1970s and '80s, and that housing is what's for sale today. These homes can be great value. They also come with predictable inspection items.

What should I test for in an older Pocono home?

Radon

Radon is a naturally occurring radioactive gas, and it's common in Pennsylvania. The state Department of Environmental Protection estimates about 40% of Pennsylvania homes have radon above the EPA action level of 4 picocuries per liter. Testing is quick, and mitigation systems are common if levels are high. Ask if the home already has a mitigation system and when it was last tested.

Well water

Many Pocono homes use a private well. Pennsylvania's DEP doesn't regulate private wells, so no agency has tested that water for you. Have it tested by a DEP-accredited lab. Common tests include coliform bacteria and nitrates, plus others your inspector or lab recommends for the area. Also ask about the well's age, depth and flow rate if known.

Septic system

A home on an on-lot septic system needs its own inspection, separate from the general home inspection. Ask the seller for pumping and repair records. A septic repair or replacement is one of the largest surprise costs a buyer can face, so this is not the place to save money.

What systems wear out in a house this age?

Heating, and any fuel tanks

Ask the age of the furnace or boiler and for service records. If the home heats with oil or propane, ask where the tank is and who owns it. Some propane tanks are leased from the supplier. An old, buried oil tank, even one no longer in use, is worth asking about before you're under contract.

Electrical

Homes from this era may have older panels or wiring that home inspectors flag for further review by an electrician, including some panel brands from the period and aluminum branch wiring used in some homes built in the mid-1960s to early 1970s. Ask your inspector to call these out specifically.

Plumbing

Depending on the age and any updates, older homes may have galvanized steel pipes or polybutylene plumbing (used from roughly the late 1970s into the mid-1990s). Both are worth identifying before you buy.

Roof, windows and moisture

Ask for the roof's age. In the Poconos, also look closely at crawl spaces, basements and grading, where moisture problems show up.

What other paperwork matters?

  • Seller's disclosure. Pennsylvania law requires sellers to disclose known material defects. Read it before you make an offer if you can, and compare it with what your inspector finds.
  • Lead-based paint disclosure. For homes built before 1978, federal rules require sellers to disclose known lead-based paint hazards and give buyers an opportunity to test.
  • Community rules and roads. Many Pocono homes sit in private communities with HOA dues, road maintenance or rental rules. Get those documents early.

Is an older Pocono home a bad investment?

No. Most of the market is older housing, and many of these homes have been well updated. The goal is to know what you're buying and price repairs into your offer or your plans, rather than finding out after closing.
